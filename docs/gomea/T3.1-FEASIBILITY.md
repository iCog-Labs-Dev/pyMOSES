## The idea

BASE-TLS (`--linear-scaling terms`, round 12) scores a program as `w_0 + Σ w_i·t_i`, with the term
weights fitted by least squares on the training rows. It has no LM: the CLI refuses
`--linear-scaling terms` with any `--const-opt` other than `none` (`problem_params.py:1728`).
T3.1 adds LM on the constants inside the terms. At each LM pass, the term weights are fitted
again by least squares, so LM tunes only the inner constants. This is the separable form of
nonlinear least squares (the linear weights are eliminated at each pass).

**Why now (round 12, `SERVER-ROUND12-FINDINGS.md`):**

* BASE-TLS was better than BASE (0.879) without any LM.
* On Bos and Yac the final programs have one term. There BASE-TLS is BASE without LM, and it lost
  on Yac (1.61). LM would give those programs back their constant tuning.
* Backlog item B4-split (Aldeia and de França, GECCO 2022 Companion) reports that per-term LM with
  least squares for the weights had the lowest or tied test NMSE of four methods on six of our
  datasets (text claims only; see `BACKLOG.md` section 8, item 7).

## What is new

1. `RegressionRows` gets a `terms` flag. With it, `residuals(tree)` evaluates the terms, fits the
   weights with `fit_terms`, and returns `fitted − y`. A one-term program, or a fit that falls
   back, uses the plain `a + b·f` residuals, as the scorer does.
2. `validation_nmse` with `terms`: the weights come from the training rows, as in `sr_eval`.
3. The CLI accepts `--linear-scaling terms` with `--const-opt lm` (and `--const-opt-guard val`).
   The other `--const-opt` modes (mutation, polish) stay refused with `terms`. `--const-opt-top-k`
   and `--const-opt-during` stay refused with `terms` (not in the scope).
4. `RegressionRows.of_bscore` reads the scorer's `terms` state, so LM and the scorer always use
   the same scaling rule.
5. Tests: LM in `terms` mode lowers the training MSE of a two-term program with an inner
   constant; a constant that a term weight absorbs (for example `c·t`) is held fixed by the
   existing inert-column rule; the guard in `terms` mode; the CLI refusals; identity tests
   (`--linear-scaling 1` with LM, and `--linear-scaling terms` without LM, are byte-identical).

## What it can break, and the risks

* **Default runs:** nothing. The new path runs only with `--linear-scaling terms` and
  `--const-opt lm` together. Identity tests prove it.
* **Cost per LM pass:** each pass evaluates the terms one by one (the same work as the whole
  program) and solves one small system (terms² ≤ 30² in round 12). This is a small extra cost.
* **Inert constants:** a constant that only scales a whole term is absorbed by its weight. The
  existing rule holds such a constant fixed (zero Jacobian column). No new rule is needed.
* **Overfitting:** more fitted values. The validation guard (LM-VAL) stays on, as in BASE.
* **Poles:** LM can move a denominator to zero, as in round 11 (Bos split 35). The pole safeguard
  arm of round 13 tests a check for this; T3.1 itself does not add one.
